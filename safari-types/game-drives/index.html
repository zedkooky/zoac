Safari Type 03

Game Drives

The classic, done well: an open vehicle, a patient guide and the freedom to follow a story as it unfolds.

Pace
Two drives a day, 3–4 hours each
Suits
Everyone, including families
Best for
Big cats and big herds
Watch for
Vehicle numbers at sightings

On this page

  1. A typical day
  2. What makes a good game drive
  3. Open or closed vehicles?
  4. Where game drives shine

Game drives are how most people experience safari, and for good reason: a vehicle lets you cover ground, get close to big animals safely and stay with a sighting as it develops. The difference between an ordinary game drive and an exceptional one comes down to a few things worth knowing before you book.

A typical day

Most camps run two drives a day. The morning drive leaves around sunrise, when predators are often still active, and returns late morning. After lunch and a rest through the heat, the afternoon drive heads out around 3.30 or 4pm and usually ends with a sundowner stop before returning after dark.

What makes a good game drive

  • The guide. More than anything else. A great guide reads behaviour, predicts what will happen and explains what you are seeing.
  • Vehicle numbers. In busy national parks, a lion sighting can attract many vehicles. Private concessions and conservancies limit how many vehicles can be at a sighting.
  • Off-road and night driving. National parks often require vehicles to stay on roads and return by dusk. Private reserves usually allow off-road driving to follow animals and spotlit night drives for nocturnal species.
  • Seats. Ask how many guests share a vehicle. Fewer guests means everyone gets a good view, and some camps offer a private vehicle.

Open or closed vehicles?

Southern African camps mostly use open-sided 4x4s. In East Africa, pop-top minibuses and closed 4x4s are common, especially on longer road transfers. Open vehicles feel more immersive; pop-tops give good standing views for photography. Both are safe as long as you stay seated and inside.

Where game drives shine

  • Serengeti, Tanzania and Maasai Mara, Kenya — big cats and the great migration.
  • Kenya’s conservancies — Mara game drives with strict vehicle limits.
  • Etosha, Namibia — waterholes in the dry season, ideal for self-drive.
  • Botswana’s private concessions — off-road, night drives and very few vehicles.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a game drive?
Usually three to four hours. Most camps run one in the early morning and one in the late afternoon, sometimes continuing into a night drive.
Are game drives good for children?
Yes. Game drives suit all ages, though some camps set minimum ages or require families to book a private vehicle.
