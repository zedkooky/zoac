Safari Types

Choose Your Kind of Safari

Five ways to be in the wild. Each changes what you see, what you hear and how close you get.

Most first-time travelers choose a country first and a style of safari second. We think it works better the other way round. The way you move through the bush — on foot, by canoe, in an open vehicle — shapes the whole experience far more than which side of a border you are on.

How to choose

Start with three questions:

  • How close do you want to be? A game drive covers ground and gets you near big animals safely. A walk or a canoe trip puts you inside the landscape, with fewer sightings but far more intensity.
  • How much comfort matters? Mobile and fly camps trade en-suite luxury for remoteness. Permanent lodges give you both, at a price.
  • How fit and how patient are you? Walking safaris suit people who enjoy slow detail. Game drives suit anyone, including children and travelers with limited mobility.

Most good itineraries combine two or three styles: a few nights of game drives to see the big animals, then a river or walking camp to slow down. If you are unsure, our first-time safari guide walks through the decision step by step.

Walking Safaris

Canoe & River Safaris

Mobile Safaris
