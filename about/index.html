Our Approach

Safari begins with understanding. We explain how it works — then you decide where to go.

On this page

  1. What we believe
  2. How we’re funded
  3. Why the Lower Zambezi
  4. How we can help

Safari.today exists because choosing a safari is harder than it should be. Most information is written to sell a particular camp or package. We wanted a place that explains how African safari actually works — the kinds of safari, the seasons, the countries, the costs — so travelers can choose with confidence.

What we believe

  • Field-written. Our guides come from time on the ground and from the guides, camp teams and conservationists who work there.
  • Independent. No paid placements. We recommend what we would choose ourselves, and we say so when something isn’t right for everyone.
  • Understanding first. How a safari works matters more than where you book it. A walking safari and a game drive in the same park are completely different trips.
  • Honest about money. We talk openly about what safaris cost and why — and how to get better value.

How we’re funded

Safari.today is free to read. When we help you plan a trip, we may earn a commission from the camps and operators we book — the same rates you would pay booking direct. No one pays to appear on this site. Safari.today is also a sister company of the Zambian Outdoor Adventure Company, which runs diving and adventure trips in Lusaka and on Lake Tanganyika; we say so wherever we mention it.

Why the Lower Zambezi

We cover all the major safari countries, but there is one place we know exceptionally well: Zambia’s Lower Zambezi. We return there more than anywhere else, and we think it shows what safari can be when it is built around a river.

How we can help

Start with our first-time safari guide, or get in touch — by email or WhatsApp — with your dates, interests and budget. We’ll give you a straight answer about what fits.
